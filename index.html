Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Indian salary calculations

Salary is a fixed amount of money or compensation paid to an employee by an employer in return for work performed. The most common components are:

Basic Salary: Fixed base part, usually 50% of gross salary. Fully taxable.
HRA (House Rent Allowance): For rented accommodation expenses. Tax-exempt if conditions are met.
LTA (Leave Travel Allowance): Covers travel expenses when on leave. Eligible for income tax exemption.
Special Allowance: Fixed amount over basic salary. Fully taxable.
Bonus: Performance-based incentive. Fully taxable.
EPF: Both employer and employee contribute 12% of basic salary. Retirement benefit scheme.
Professional Tax: State-level tax, max ₹2,500/year.
CTC (Cost to Company) is the total annual cost your employer spends to hire you, including benefits like employer EPF, gratuity, insurance, etc.

In-hand salary is what you actually receive after deductions like Employee EPF, Income Tax, and Professional Tax.

Your CTC will always be higher than your in-hand salary. For example, a ₹10 LPA CTC typically gives you around ₹70,000–₹75,000/month in-hand.
Step 1: Gross Salary = CTC − Employer's EPF − Gratuity

Step 2: Taxable Income = Gross Salary − Standard Deduction − HRA Exemption − 80C Deductions (varies by regime)

Step 3: Calculate Income Tax based on applicable slab rates + 4% Cess

Step 4: Take-Home = Gross Salary − Income Tax − Employee EPF − Professional Tax

Gratuity is calculated as: (15/26) × Monthly Basic Salary × Years of Service
HRA (House Rent Allowance) is a monetary benefit for rented accommodation expenses. The tax-exempt portion is the minimum of:

1. Actual HRA received from employer
2. Actual rent paid minus 10% of basic salary
3. 50% of basic salary (metro city) or 40% (non-metro city)

Note: HRA exemption is only available under the Old Tax Regime. If you don't pay rent, HRA is fully taxable.
Both employer and employee contribute 12% of basic salary towards EPF.

If basic salary ≤ ₹15,000/month: 12% of basic salary (mandatory)
If basic salary > ₹15,000/month: Company can choose to contribute either 12% of ₹15,000 (₹1,800/month) or 12% of full basic salary

Employee EPF contribution qualifies for Section 80C deduction under the old regime (up to ₹1.5 lakh limit).
The New Tax Regime (post Budget 2025) is the default regime for FY 2026-27:

• Up to ₹4L = Nil   • ₹4-8L = 5%   • ₹8-12L = 10%
• ₹12-16L = 15%   • ₹16-20L = 20%   • ₹20-24L = 25%   • Above ₹24L = 30%

Key features: Standard deduction of ₹75,000. Section 87A rebate gives zero tax for taxable income up to ₹12 lakh (effectively ₹12.75 lakh with standard deduction). Marginal relief available for income slightly above ₹12 lakh.
There's no one-size-fits-all answer. It depends on your investments and deductions:

Choose New Regime if: You don't have significant investments (80C, 80D), don't pay rent, or your CTC is below ₹12.75 LPA.

Choose Old Regime if: You have ₹1.5L+ in 80C investments, pay substantial rent (HRA exemption), have medical insurance (80D), and NPS contributions.

Pro tip: Use our calculator to compare both regimes — it shows you which one saves more!
Gratuity is a monetary benefit given by an employer for services rendered. You're eligible after completing 5 years of continuous service (exception: death or disability).

Formula: Gratuity = (Last Basic Salary + DA) × 15/26 × Completed Years of Service

While gratuity is typically paid as a lump sum at the end of service, the annual set-aside (15/26 × Monthly Basic) is deducted from your CTC for calculation purposes.
Marginal relief prevents excessive taxation when income is just above the tax-free limit (₹12 lakh for FY 2026-27).

How it works: Your tax payable won't exceed the amount by which your income exceeds ₹12 lakh.

Examples:
• ₹12.10L income → Tax = ₹10,000 (not ₹61,500)
• ₹12.50L income → Tax = ₹50,000 (not ₹67,500)
• ₹12.70L income → Tax = ₹70,000 (not ₹70,500)
• ₹12.75L income → Tax = ₹71,250 (no relief applies above this)
Financial Year (FY): The year in which income is earned (April to March). Example: FY 2026-27.

Assessment Year (AY): The following year when tax is evaluated and returns are filed. Example: AY 2027-28.

So income earned during FY 2026-27 is assessed and tax returns filed in AY 2027-28. The Income Tax Act, 2025 introduces a unified "Tax Year" concept intended to simplify this.
